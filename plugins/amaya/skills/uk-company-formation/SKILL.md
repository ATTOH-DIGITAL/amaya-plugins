# UK Company Formation

Help the user set up a UK business correctly, in three steps: **(1) choose the structure → (2) incorporate → (3) work the post-formation compliance calendar.**

> This is general information, not legal, tax, or accounting advice. Figures and thresholds change — tell the user to confirm current rates on GOV.UK and check their own situation with a qualified accountant or solicitor.

## 1. Choose the structure

| Structure | Liability | How it's taxed | Privacy | Best when |
|-----------|-----------|----------------|---------|-----------|
| **Sole trader** | Unlimited (personal) | Income Tax + Class 2/4 NI via Self Assessment | Name not on a public register | Testing an idea, low risk, simple |
| **Limited company (Ltd)** | Limited to the company | Corporation Tax; you take salary + dividends | Directors/PSCs public at Companies House | Most growth/SME cases — credibility, tax planning, limited liability |
| **LLP** | Limited, but members taxed personally | Members taxed as self-employed | Members public | Professional partnerships (law, accountancy) |
| **Ordinary partnership** | Unlimited, shared | Each partner via Self Assessment | Not registered | Two+ people, low-risk, simple |

Decision shortcuts:
- Want limited liability + a credible entity to invoice/raise from → **Ltd**.
- Solo, low risk, minimal admin, keep it simple → **sole trader** (you can incorporate later).
- Regulated profession with partners → **LLP**.

## 2. Incorporate a private limited company (Ltd)

Everything files with **Companies House**. Have ready:

1. **Company name** — run a name availability check; avoid "sensitive" words and trademarks.
2. **Registered office** — a UK address that becomes public; must receive official mail. (Many founders use a registered-office service to keep their home address private.)
3. **Director(s)** — at least one, 16+. Service address is public; residential address is kept private.
4. **Shareholder(s) + share structure** — even a one-person company needs ≥1 share. Decide number of shares, share class, and nominal value (e.g. 100 ordinary shares at £0.01 or £1).
5. **People with Significant Control (PSC)** — anyone holding >25% shares/votes or controlling the company. Goes on the public PSC register.
6. **SIC code(s)** — pick the code(s) describing what the business does.
7. **Memorandum & Articles of Association** — standard model articles are fine for most.
8. **File IN01** (online is fastest) and pay the Companies House fee (check the current amount on GOV.UK). The **Certificate of Incorporation** is usually issued within ~24 hours online.

## 3. After incorporation — the first 90 days

- **Register for Corporation Tax** with HMRC within **3 months** of starting to trade.
- **Business bank account** — open one in the company name (keep company and personal money separate).
- **VAT** — register if taxable turnover exceeds the registration threshold (check the current figure on GOV.UK), or voluntarily if it helps.
- **PAYE** — register before the first payday if you'll pay yourself a salary or employ anyone.
- **Accounting records + accounting reference date** — set up bookkeeping from day one.
- **Confirmation Statement** — file at least once every 12 months to confirm company details (a small annual fee).
- **Annual accounts** — first accounts are due 21 months after incorporation; then 9 months after each financial year-end. **Corporation Tax return + payment** run on their own deadlines.

### Key recurring deadlines

| Obligation | Cadence |
|------------|---------|
| Confirmation Statement | Annually |
| Annual accounts (Companies House) | Yearly, from your accounting reference date |
| Corporation Tax return (CT600) | Yearly |
| VAT returns (if registered) | Usually quarterly |
| Payroll / RTI (if PAYE) | Each pay run |
